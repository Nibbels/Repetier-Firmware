## Host contract and wire format
Official [Host configuration](https://www.repetier.com/documentation/repetier-host/rh-installation-and-configuration/) and [Server communication errors](https://www.repetier-server.com/knowledgebase/4012/) document ping-pong and byte-credit input buffer modes, typically 127 bytes, ACK releases credit; Server also offers max concurrent commands. Numbered ACKs can identify lost ACKs. Server timeout recovery can wrongly release credit if a command was merely slow; busy messages and appropriate timeout matter. Reconnect may reset via DTR. The [upstream binary format](https://github.com/repetier/RepetierFirmware/blob/master/repetier%20communication%20protocol.txt) documents shorter binary V1/V2 and Fletcher modulus 255. This is documented vendor behavior, NOT measured installed host source or packet trace. Confirm exact host version, whether binary is enabled/negotiated, wire byte accounting, zero sync, resend+ok, duplicate/numbered ACK, timeout and reconnect against real host software in a software-only serial endpoint; do not connect to printer. 63 and 127 are comparison windows, not a recommendation to raise actual buffer setting.

### FIFO latency
`uart-model-results.json`: isolated blackout, continuous RX and synthetic TX, preceding serviced stream, conservative FIFO full loss at back-to-back start. 500k: 594 cycles gives 0/320 phases with loss, max read response 625 cycles; 640 cycles 32/320, 800 cycles 192/320, 960 cycles 320/320. These are deterministic swept offsets, not error probabilities or guaranteed safe limits. Occupancy and pending interrupts can substantially reduce margin. Aim for one-frame response (320 cycles) where feasible; include UDR read offset in deadlines, and require zero FIFO overflow under all accepted workloads.

`priority-model-results.json`: synthetic non-nested priority sensitivity; Timer0 COMPB nominal period 4096, Timer0 OVF nominal 16384 assuming /64, WDT nominal 256000. Other periods and all non-UART costs synthetic, not real measurements. At 500k, TX offered 1/4 of RX, 400 RX bytes, 32 sampled phases: light 0/32 loss, max RX read 563 cycles; medium 32/32 with worst 21 bytes lost, max 1196 cycles; heavy masked 32/32 with worst 290, max 6445 cycles, Advance flag coalescence and TX backlog. Medium demonstrates that average utilization below 100% is not sufficient. No claim these are RFx000 production profiles. Actual Timer1 nesting and rescheduling, Advance edge timing, PWM path/ADC/UI/RGB and stack remain to measure. Finite workload allows TX backlog; no proof of sustainable physical TX.

### Pacing
`pacing-model-results.json`: 1000 commands, 500k, synthetic 1ms host delay, 200us parser service, 500us parsed-command execution, 6-byte ACK. 19-byte frame: pingpong 625.39 cmd/s, 63 credit 1250, 127 credit 1715.27. 40-byte frame: 500.25/500.25/833.33. No CPU/physical FIFO/actual motion planning here. ## Loss characterization and proposed contract
`loss-replay.py` adds six baseline failure-characterization scenarios to copied host harness: wrapped RX ring drops a digit, FE, UPE (synthetic because 8N1), DOR, two equal-byte deletion under XOR, 0x00->0xff Fletcher collision with FE. Checksum collisions are algebraically confirmed; C++ result pending. These are intended to show baseline failures, not green acceptance of a repair. In later integration convert to positive no-corrupt-admission checks, keep good frames and prior queue tests.

`recovery-contract.js` runs 15 abstract scenarios: four loss categories at three admission boundaries, unnumbered abort, repeated/saturating flags, stale boundary proof invalidation after loss during sync. Actual protocol synchronization is deliberately an external oracle. This is not a proposed wire protocol.

Implementation sketch after P0 and manager window:
1. Preserve SPSC ownership. Read status then data early in RX ISR; capture FE/DOR/UPE and SW full independently. On first loss close a sticky gate and discard subsequent untrusted stream until controlled recovery. No ISR prints, EEPROM or blocking. Counters saturate; DOR count = observed overrun flags not missing-byte count. Compare single-byte ISR vs bounded already-ready two-byte drain; no waiting, no naked ISR absent ABI/stack proof.
2. Before parsing, snapshot gate. Parse into uncommitted command/session candidate. Before any line state, queue publication, M110/M112 or other side effects, atomically recheck gate/transaction validity and commit only short state changes. No parsing or serial logging while interrupts disabled. If loss after a committed command but before ACK, preserve ordered ACK intent then recovery intent; avoid duplicate execution. Conservative invalidation of an otherwise valid uncommitted frame is acceptable only with a proven resend path. Preserve borrowed text ownership and valid queued commands unless safe abort policy explicitly requires discarding. No false ACK for damaged frame.
3. In recovery keep gate closed, clear untrusted partial/ring state atomically with producer coordination, separate diagnostic consumption from gate reopening. Numbered stream: request next last *committed* number, verify actual Repetier resend+ok and synchronization handshake including arbitrary binary zeros, already in-flight bytes, repeated loss, line wrap and ACK loss. Reopening on a zero run alone is not justified. Recheck no new loss between verified boundary and reopen, avoid generation wrap; sticky handshake is a candidate. If unnumbered/ambiguous stream cannot replay, safe job stop/explicit reconnection rather than guessing. Maintain fail-safe emergency behavior even during quarantine; transport uncertainty is not grounds to suppress safety action. Exact recovery and stop policy remain integration blockers. Unflagged wire corruption can collide with legacy checksums; cannot guarantee detection of all corruption without stronger end-to-end protocol.
4. Only after transport correctness compare costs (successful, full, each flag, bounded drain, empty UDRE), stack/nesting, TX stalls and P0 deadlines. Reuse actual ELF, source hashes and Task0032 combined workloads; compare all four board variants and compiler settings. 500k target, 250k fallback candidate; no automatic baud change.

### Engineering timing contract

| Quantity | Target CPU cycles | At 16MHz |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Nominal full UART frame | 640 | 40 us |
| RX normal ISR incl. entry/exit | <=80 | <=5 us |
| RX loss/error ISR incl. entry/exit | <=100 | <=6.25 us |
| TX ordinary data ISR incl. entry/exit | <=80 | <=5 us |
| TX empty ISR incl. entry/exit (if retained) | <=64 | <=4 us |
| IRQ entry through UDR read complete | <=40 | <=2.5 us |
| Total release/RX-ready to UDR read complete | <=512 | <=32 us |
| Combined pre-entry/dispatch/interference allowance if read path takes 40 | <=472 | <=29.5 us |
| Nominal reserve to next frame | 128 | 8 us |

These are explicit **engineering budgets**, not measured integrated WCET, achieved acceptance or permission to violate other deadlines. Isolated linked checked/cached candidate achieved 77 cycles normal RX, read complete at 33, structural error paths 67/80, TX 71, empty TX 61, stack RX12/TX11 including 3-byte return. Start with one byte per RX interrupt (simplest to bound), checked status before UDR, cached ring indexes; no mandatory drain2, naked ISR, or aggressive TX-last optimization. Any gate, fault counter or recovery addition must be linked/recounted. Historical 594-cycle noninterruptible sample + checked read offset 33 = 627 cycles before any other delay: only 13 cycles nominal margin, exceeds 512 target. Aim for <=400-cycle contiguous nonpreemptible sections where feasible; NOT a universal cap or sufficient condition. With a 40-cycle read path this leaves 72 cycles of the target for other pre-entry delays; actual arbitration, residual instruction latency, pending ISR/SEI/RETI deferral, nesting and previously queued FIFO bytes must be modeled. The one-frame service condition is sufficient against accumulation for a clean stream starting with no backlog if each byte is read before the next arrives; use actual shortest permitted frame interval incl. oscillator tolerance, prove occupancy and no hardware/SW loss for accepted burst scenarios, and handle loss deterministically. Do not treat 640 or any 2-byte FIFO multiplier as universal allowable blackout; initial occupancy and recurring service windows matter. Mandatory heater, watchdog, STEP/DIR and Advance deadlines take precedence. An infeasible combined scenario must limit workload or improve scheduling; do not extend unsafe mask windows.

At saturation budgeted RX uses 80/640 = 12.5% CPU; simultaneous full-rate TX another 12.5%, excluding empty IRQs/parser/other services. At equal absolute byte rates, UART work is independent of selected baud to first order. Existing 127/63 usable RX/TX ring capacities give 5.08ms RX fill from empty and 2.52ms TX drain from full at line rate, excluding hardware TX pipeline and foreground delays; these are not universal service deadlines. Retain ring sizes initially. Require host-credit/ACK accounting and bounded foreground/TX behavior before SRAM allocation changes. P0 currently adds tryWrite/tryWriteSpan/transmitFree; preserve these and emergency behavior in integration.

### Workload contract and segment envelope

Reproduce: `node tools/task0033/transport-contract.js`; compare to `transport-contract-results.json` in task-private CI. The script has eight assertions and 45 cases (3 frame sizes x 5 lengths x 3 requested speeds). This is arithmetic, NOT a new combined simulator or measured real print throughput. For sustained all-motion stream, command rate lambda=v/L and byte demand=lambda*B (B actual RX wire bytes per move, including line/checksum/framing). For a mixture use total bytes per delivered path distance over a relevant mutable horizon, with non-motion traffic counted separately. The normal planning allowance is 80% of 25000 bytes/s (20000 bytes/s), deliberately chosen as a first-pass reserve, not a safety guarantee or fixed governor parameter. Actual host/parse/planner/IRQ constraints may lower sustainable rate. ACK traffic travels in opposite direction, contributes CPU and host pacing but should not simply be subtracted from RX wire bandwidth.

| Actual RX bytes/move | Ideal moves/s | Planning moves/s | Mean L at 20 mm/s | Mean L at 60 mm/s | Mean L at 100 mm/s |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 20 | 1250 | 1000 | 0.020 mm | 0.060 mm | 0.100 mm |
| 40 | 625 | 500 | 0.040 mm | 0.120 mm | 0.200 mm |
| 60 | 416.67 | 333.33 | 0.060 mm | 0.180 mm | 0.300 mm |

Lengths in table are planning-budget boundaries for sustained uniform segment streams, not minimum legal segment lengths. Absolute wire thresholds at 40B/move: 0.032/0.096/0.160mm at 20/60/100mm/s. E.g. 0.1mm and 60mm/s = 600 moves/s, 24k B/s, 96% wire; within ideal limit but not comfortably below it. 0.2mm and 100mm/s =500 moves/s, 20k B/s, within planning allowance, not a guarantee of CPU/physical speed. 0.05mm and 20mm/s =400 moves/s, 16k B/s; at 60mm/s =1200 moves/s,48k B/s, impossible for 40B frames at 250k unless buffered transient; desired steady-state cap 25mm/s planning,31.25mm/s ideal. 0.025mm at 40B yields 12.5mm/s planning,15.625mm/s absolute. Do not drop, merge, or modify G-code geometry in firmware just because a segment is short. Rare short segments may consume queue reserve safely; sustained dense regions need advance braking and a filtered local supply limit, not a discontinuous per-line speed clamp. Preserve user request and acceleration/jerk/thermal/STEP limits. Task0032 to choose control policy and prove lookahead/braking, not Task0033.

Slicer defaults constrain contour error, not actual output-length distributions; see Task0033 owner decision and citations. Based on that geometry analysis, 0.1–0.5mm are useful normal workload probes, 0.05mm dense and 0.025mm severe stress, no universal empirical frequency claim. Use 0.2mm as first common 40B/100mm/s workload at planning margin, 0.1mm as near-wire at 60mm/s, 0.05mm as intentional slowdown at 60/100. Validate actual traces when available (length quantiles, bytes/mm, longest dense runs, non-motion commands, mixed lengths).
