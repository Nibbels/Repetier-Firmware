## Objective and priorities

Preserve configured physical microstepping and actual commanded microstep counts. Closely bunched pulses can mechanically resemble coarser microstepping, although the driver still executes the configured microsteps. Prefer uniform temporal spacing for surface quality, quietness, resonance and path accuracy. Use the smallest feasible packing group, and pack only as necessary to preserve processor capacity for planning, serial, temperature, ADC, DMS, compensation, UI and watchdog. Optimize highest sustainable print speed at this quality/safety objective, not maximum instantaneous pulse rate or maximum packing. Owner wants to eliminate manually tuned DblFq switching thresholds and their menu setting after a justified automatic policy is verified. Do not change driver microsteps as an automatic CPU load control.

## Source findings / baseline

- Configuration.h: XY=32, Z=16, E=32 microsteps; nominal XY=152.38 steps/mm (4.761875*32), Z=1280, E=280 RF1000/RF2000 or 256 RF2000v2. Actual EEPROM-calibrated values and all supported microstep configurations matter. At 32 XY, straight 40/60/100/150 mm/s requests ~6095/9143/15238/22857 primary steps/s. Diagonal XY at same vector speed has ~0.707 times the primary frequency but potentially two emitted XY pulses per iteration. E rate is filament mm/s times steps/mm, not XY feedrate times E calibration. E may be primary; Advance/ECMP can add output work.
- MotionPlanner::prepareMove uses requested duration, max axis feedrates and LIMIT_INTERVAL=((F_CPU/40000)+1)=401 at 16 MHz; StepExecutor separately clamps CPUDivU2(v) to 512 ticks (32 us, nominal 31250 base primary steps/s) before DigitFlow intervalScale multiplication/shift. Neither is a measured minimum wall-time ISR interval nor a utilization/deadline proof. Do not remove, loosen, or conflate either floor with packing; audit effect of scaling, timer convention, ramp clock and actual physical rate. Preserve guard pending proof. HAL setTimer late compare stretches short intervals to TCNT1+100; distinguish late recovery from sustainable scheduling. Long compare split, sentinel/wrap and pending compare flags must be tested.
- Current group n=max(1,ceil(3300/T)) using effective integer interval T, configurable 3300..5000. At nominal default, straight XY packing starts ~31.8 mm/s; ideal 40/60/100/150 mm/s groups 2/2/4/5. Task0027 optimized the arithmetic, not switching policy. Actual threshold transitions depend on legacy CPUDivU2 interpolation, floor and DigitFlow. Current timer1 group drives a common Bresenham primary timeline: max axis step count selects primary, but total per-axis work, pulse density, endstop paths, compensation and Advance determine CPU cost. Axis-specific workload classes are sensible; independently grouping XY axes would change path timing/geometry and is not an initial candidate.
- Timer1 masks own OCIE1A before allowing selected nested services; other ISR owners must not reenter themselves or create recursive stacks. AVR executes one instruction stream, not true parallel IRQs. Preserve interrupt mask/restore ownership and STEP/DIR low/high, endstop, pressure and abort semantics. No blanket sei in ISRs or blanket cli across whole packs. Timer0 COMPA is independent Advance E pulse owner, COMPA service is demand-driven; Timer1 feeds pending E/lead/ECMP. Count actual E pulses, pending backlog and direction transitions, not merely timer capacity. Preserve 31-cycle production Advance HIGH at nominal 16 MHz, no below-31 experiment without separate owner approval.
- Timer0 COMPB software heater/fan PWM and ADC service ~3906.25/s (256 us); core Timer0 overflow ~976.56/s; WDT interrupt ~62.5/s; UART0 nominal 115200 8N1 11520 B/s each direction possible, RX usable 127 B, TX 63. Current scanTick ~10 ms, feature ~50 ms, temperature ~100 ms, compensation ~16 ms. These are nominal schedules, not proven service deadlines. Events are bools and can coalesce. Commands::checkForPeriodicalActions refreshes heartbeat on scanTick before thermal work; WDT interrupt-only and toggles external watchdog if heartbeat <20000 ms. Neither heartbeat nor lack of reset proves temperature control has completed or outputs fail off. Review independent fail-off, WDI wiring/revision, reset bias, stale ADC, thermal completion and watchdog coverage with docs/heater-safety.md. Owner reports historical crashes with heater PWM stuck high and smoking/glowing hardware. Safety is a hard gate, not a throughput trade. Do not shorten/disable watchdog or alter heater PWM without separate scoped safety proof.
- Current nextStepSpeed updates once per group; clock.ticks += interval*pulses (base interval, not measured wall time); Z compensation waitSteps=mass+stepsPerTimerCall couples correction cadence to packing. Changing group selection also changes ramp discretization, pulse bunching and Advance lead updates, group clipping at move end, half-interval handoffs and Z correction. Compare trajectories/timing/compensation, not only final step counts.
- Task0030 isolated fixture worst utilization 38.4%/59.7%/97.4%/136.3%/170.4% at 4/8/16/24/31.25k primary steps/s with previous packing; NOT concurrent WCET or actual print speed. Dense E/Advance/ECMP can exceed available time even with packing. Separate costs common to IRQ invocation from per-primary-iteration and per-physical-axis-pulse costs. Reuse Task0030 artifact/interpreter ZIP and actual-source replays; archive input hashes; a restricted interpreter is not hardware simulation.

## Phase A — measure budget before choosing thresholds

1. Freeze Task0030 reference and build exact candidate all four boards. Count full Timer1 dispatch incl. entry/exit, setTimer, activation/completion/empty/wait, ramp/period, per-step and per-axis cost, min/max masked windows and nested IRQ response. Sweep groups 1..legacy maximum and representative alternatives, straight X/Y, diagonal XY, varying E ratio including E primary, Advance off/on, ECMP, nominal Z and compensation, travel/retract/homing, reversal/limit, direct/queued and move boundaries, short/long segments; every tool/board, configured calibration and relevant microsteps. Keep geometry, feedrate, acceleration, step counts and workload identical. Map realistic slicer paths (e.g. 30/40/60/80/100/120/150 mm/s) to actual axis and E rates; include volumetric E demand. Characterize fixed cost C0, per-primary cost Cs and axis density-dependent bounds; report measured samples vs proven bounds, actual compare periods and late scheduling. Compare no packing and legacy 3300/5000. No hardware claim based on isolated traces.
2. Measure foreground workload separately: parser/ACK/line rate, prepareMove, lookahead and publication, queue starvation and lookahead depth, temperature/PID and ADC publication, DMS/TWI, Z compensation, UI/RGB and periodic checks. Determine actual execution demand and end-to-end service completion gaps, not simply main-loop iterations. Separate foreground exclusive CPU cycles from elapsed wall-time with IRQs; distinguish blocked/waiting time. Use linked instruction counts/host workload models first, task-private instrumentation design with verified unused pins or free-running timer counter without new high-frequency ISR; account for instrumentation overhead, wraps, pending/coalesced ticks. External logic analyzer and dummy-load/device tests only after explicit owner approval. Collect distributions, maxima and overload traces. Count RX DOR vs ring full, queue time coverage, pending Advance work, late timer compares, missed service deadlines, heater fall delay and watchdog heartbeat/thermal completion. Budget both utilization and worst-case response/blackout; mean idle time alone is insufficient. Until real data available, run conservative sensitivity sweeps over explicit foreground reserve and IRQ loads, label assumptions; no arbitrary final reserve percentage.
3. Demand model for each motion class and candidate n: U_timer1 approximately f_primary*(C0(n)/n+Cs)/F_CPU, plus actual other IRQ demand and foreground cost/reserve. Use per-axis step density and correction/Advance work, actual segment/ramp distribution and response-time/jitter constraints; don't use max(axis rate) alone or double-count nested ISR time. If per-step demand itself exceeds capacity, bigger packs cannot solve it; reduce admissible primary rate coherently in planner/executor, preserving geometry and E ratio. Emergency fallback behavior must not depend on delayed main loop; overload cannot cause unbounded ISR recursion/backlog or starving heater services. Separate proposed rate limiting from current behavior and validate transitions.

## Phase B — compare policies

- A: empirically justified single internal fixed threshold / group lookup. Simple predictable baseline.
- B: measured per-group switching thresholds, perhaps per motion class (X/Y vs XY, XYE, E-dense/Advance, Z/correction; use actual calibrated rates and safe conservative bins). Precompute cheap Flash lookup/decision rules; no float, expensive divide or unbounded feedback in the hot ISR. Prefer choose once per motion/interval change where safe; budget selection overhead, don't add SRAM to each of 24 slots without justification.
- C: bounded hybrid adaptive policy: smallest feasible n from static safe envelope, optional slow foreground-informed load telemetry or hysteresis, conservative ISR-local late/overload guard. Adaptation cannot wait for starvation to occur, assume average spare time is a hard deadline, or rely solely on foreground publishing a recovery command. Limit group length, temporal unevenness, rate changes, reentry, deadline misses. Compare feedback cost, stability and ramp/compensation cadence; static sufficient solution preferred over complexity.
- D (optional research only): reduce per-step costs via specialized hot paths, narrow Bresenham with range proof, distribute pulses more evenly if affordable; avoid speculative independent axis clocks / timer remapping, no safety/pin ownership change without proof. Quality objective may favor cheaper one-step execution over larger groups. Compare implementation and policy as separate commits.
- For every candidate, score smallest safe group / packed fraction, inter-step interval distribution and displacement error, motion throughput, CPU utilization and minimum foreground/service reserve, max IRQ-disabled interval, latency and thermal/watchdog deadlines, E/Z synchronization, Flash/SRAM/stack. Guard hard constraints first, then minimize packing/jitter at requested rate; if infeasible, reduce rate rather than discard steps or sacrifice safety. Measure surface quality physically before claiming improvement. Hysteresis/thresholds must not cause chatter or abrupt speed jumps.
