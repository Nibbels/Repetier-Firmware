### Timing / atomicity (linked AVR machine code)

Compared per-symbol ranges from symbols.txt and disassembly.txt (avr-nm / avr-objdump -d -S -C) of baseline and stage 1, stage 2 and final artifacts. Normalize branch destinations to local offsets and external symbol names; normalize relocated SRAM absolute operands. Inspect instruction sequence, branch topology and widths, including skip targets. Driver transfer `drv8711Transmit`: all 38 instructions byte-identical in all four builds, hence no change in that routine's instruction-path cycles. `setTimer`: 33 instructions byte-identical from stage 2 to final. Timer1 COMPA (__vector_17): 556 instructions each; Timer0 COMPA (__vector_21): 117/142/142/117; Timer0 COMPB (__vector_22): 322/401/401/398; Timer0 OVF (__vector_23): 56 each. Stage 1 normalized instruction streams identical to baseline for these and tested step helpers; from stage 2 to final same opcode/register sequence and control flow, apart from relocated immediate addresses (LDI/SUBI, one cycle each). No added ISR instructions in these routines. StepExecutor::performMove: 1410->1407 / 1433->1430 / 1433->1430 / 1392->1389. Stage 1->2 aligned differences: removed push/pop of one register (2+2 cycles) and unused volatile Advance flag load (2 cycles if packing path reached); register reallocation, same branch topology and effective packing loop. On comparable paths -4 or -6 cycles; no increased packing-loop cost. adjustDirections, stepSlowedZCompensation and updateAdvanceSteps instruction counts unchanged. This is static comparison of named routines, not full-system WCET or physical waveform measurement.

Final setter in all four variants: MOVW; IN SREG; CLI; STS high; STS low; OUT SREG; RET. CLI through restoring SREG is six instruction cycles (0.375 us at 16 MHz), includes both two-cycle stores, and does not unconditionally enable interrupts. Timer1 is sole interrupt reader, no ISR writer, so no torn two-byte threshold. Existing driver interrupt protection and panel rescale protection retained. No timing claim for unrelated foreground paths or physical pin propagation.
